Prompt Template, Part 7

Answer only from what you give it

Read after Part 6. The AI knows a lot, and it invents what it does not know. Grounding fixes that: give it your source, tell it to answer only from that source, and tell it to say when the answer is not there.

The idea

What grounding is

Grounding means curating what the model sees. You paste the actual source into the prompt: the notes, the price list, the letter, the policy. Then you fence the answer in: use only this source, quote it where you can, and say "I do not know" if the answer is not in it.

The one rule: if it is not in your source, the AI must not say it. A missing answer is better than an invented one.

The three parts of a grounded prompt

  • The source. The real text, pasted into the prompt, labeled so the AI knows what it is.
  • The fence. "Answer only from the source below." One line that tells the AI where its facts may come from.
  • The escape. "If the answer is not in the source, say you do not know." Without this line, the AI fills gaps with guesses.

Grounding pairs with Part 3. Part 3's no-invention rule guards the output. Grounding guards the input. Together, they keep the answer tied to your material on both sides.

How to do it well

Ground well: small source, clear fence

  • Paste the actual source, not a summary of it. A summary you wrote can already carry your mistakes. The real text is the ground truth.
  • Keep the source small. Paste the smallest piece that holds the answer: one page, one list, one email thread. A huge pile of text buries the answer and costs context.
  • Name the source. "Source: the price list below." A label tells the AI what the pasted text is and what it is for.
  • Ask for quotes. "Quote the exact line that supports your answer." A quote you can check is worth more than a smooth paragraph you cannot.
  • Check the source, not just the answer. Grounding on a wrong source gives a wrong answer with confidence. If the source is unreliable, say so in the prompt, or find a better one.
No source, no fence“Summarize what we decided about the budget.” The AI has no notes to work from, so it invents a decision that sounds reasonable and may be entirely wrong.
Source plus fence plus escape“Source: the meeting notes below. Answer only from these notes. If the answer is not in the notes, say you do not know. What did we decide about the budget?”
Source, but no quotes“Here is our price list. What does the large cake cost?” The AI may give a price from memory instead of the list, and you cannot tell which one it used.
Source, fence, and a quote“Here is our price list. What does the large cake cost? Quote the exact line from the list that gives the price.” The quote proves the answer came from the list.

Answer questions from a handout

The job: You have a one page handout about a class trip, and a parent asks three questions: the date, the cost, and what to bring. Answer only from the handout.

Source, fence, escape

SourceThe trip handout. Trip date: Saturday, May 16. Cost: $12 per student. Bring: water bottle, hat, and lunch.
FenceAnswer only from the handout. Quote the exact line that supports each answer.
EscapeIf a question is not answered in the handout, say so.

Visual 2 of 5 • Summary

Summarize a letter using only its words

The job: You have a letter from your landlord about repairs, and you want a short summary for your records. The summary must not add anything the letter does not say.

Source, fence, escape

Source“The roof will be repaired the week of June 8. Rent stays the same. Please report any leaks to the office.”
FenceSummarize in three sentences, using only what the letter says. Do not add dates, promises, or details that are not in the letter. Keep the dates exact.
EscapeIf a detail is not in the letter, leave it out.

What to notice: Read the summary against the letter, line by line. If the summary adds a promise the letter never made, the fence needs to be stronger, not the summary longer.

Visual 3 of 5 • Checking

Check a claim against a source

The job: A friend says a store is open on Sundays. You have the store hours list. Check the claim against the list, and say what the list actually says.

Claim, source, fence

ClaimThe store is open on Sundays.
SourceStore hours. Monday to Saturday: 9 am to 6 pm. Sunday: closed.
FenceCheck this claim using only the store hours. Quote the exact line that settles it.

The verdict

What the claim saysThe store is open on Sundays.
What the source says“Sunday: closed.” The claim is false, and the line proves it.

What to notice: Try a claim the list does not cover, like holiday hours, and check that the AI says it does not know.

Visual 4 of 5 • Email

Write a reply based on an email thread

The job: A customer asks about a delivery date in an email thread. You want a reply that uses only what the thread says: the order date, the promised date, and the tracking note.

Source, fence, escape

SourceThe email thread. Order placed: May 2. Promised delivery: May 9. Tracking note: package left the warehouse May 5.
FenceUse only the dates in the thread. Do not promise a new date. Do not invent a tracking number.
EscapeIf the thread does not say it, do not write it.

What to notice: The reply should give the promised date, May 9, and the tracking note, and nothing more. If it adds a new delivery promise, the fence failed, and the constraint needs to be clearer.

Visual 5 of 5 • Your turn

Ground your own repeating job

The job: Pick one task you do with an LLM at least once a week where the answer must come from your material: your notes, your price list, your emails, your documents. Build the grounded version of its prompt, then save it.

Your grounded prompt, as a routine

  1. Pick the jobOne task where the answer must come from your material: your notes, your price list, your emails, your documents.
  2. Paste the sourceThe smallest piece that holds the answer, labeled so the AI knows what it is.
  3. Add the fence“Answer only from this source. Quote the exact line that supports your answer.”
  4. Add the escape“If the answer is not in the source, say you do not know.”
  5. Check every claim against the sourceIf the answer ever adds something the source does not say, strengthen the fence before you use it again.

Before you trust it

Grounding checklist, for every prompt

  • Did I paste the actual source, not a summary of it?
  • Did I label the source so the AI knows what it is?
  • Did I keep the source small, the smallest piece that holds the answer?
  • Did I say "answer only from this source"?
  • Did I say what to do when the answer is not there: say you do not know?
  • Did I ask for quotes, so I can check each claim?

Remember: grounding is only as good as the source. A confident answer from a wrong source is still a wrong answer.

Common failure modes

  • “Source too long and unfocused”: a huge paste buries the answer. Fix: cut the source to the smallest piece that holds it.
  • “No escape line”: the AI fills gaps with guesses because you never told it to say it does not know. Fix: always add the escape.
  • “Trusting an answer that quotes nothing”: a smooth paragraph with no quotes may come from memory, not your source. Fix: ask for quotes.
  • “Grounding on an unreliable source”: the source itself is wrong, outdated, or a guess. Fix: check the source before you paste it.
  • “A summary instead of the source”: your summary already carries your mistakes. Fix: paste the real text.

Reference

Glossary: grounding terms

Short, plain definitions for the new terms used on this page. The Part 2 glossary covers Context, Role, Goal, and Constraints.

Grounding
Curating what the AI sees, so its answers come from a source you provide.
Source
The real text you paste into a prompt, such as notes, a price list, or a letter.
Context
The material you give the AI in the prompt, including the source and your instructions.
Citation
A pointer to where an answer came from, such as a line number or a section name.
Quote
The exact words from the source, copied word for word into the answer.
Retrieval
Finding and pulling the right source material before the AI writes its answer.
Hallucination
A confident AI answer that is invented, including fake facts, quotes, or links.
Fidelity
How closely an answer sticks to the source, without adding or dropping details.
Scope
The limits you set on an answer: which source it may use, and what it may not add.
Unverified
A claim no source backs up yet, so it should not be treated as true.
Primary source
The original document itself, not someone's summary or description of it.
Claim
One statement an answer makes, which a quote from the source can support or fail to support.
