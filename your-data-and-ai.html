Your Data and AI

What happens to what you type?

When you type into an AI chat, your words do not stay on your device. They travel to company servers, where they may be stored, reviewed, and used to train future models. This page explains what happens in plain terms, which settings matter, and how to choose tools that treat your data the way you want.

Start here

Where your words go

A chat with an AI feels private. It is not a conversation in your room. It is a message sent to a company's computers.

  • Stored. Most tools keep your chats on their servers, even the free ones.
  • Reviewed. Some chats are read by people, to check safety and improve the tool.
  • Trained on. Some tools use your chats to train future models, unless you turn that off.
  • Kept after deleting. Deleting a chat does not always delete the data right away. Companies may keep copies for a time.

None of this is secret, but it is rarely explained in plain words. That is what this page does.

Free vs paid

Free tools, paid tools, and the trade

Free AI tools have to pay their bills somehow. Often, the trade is your data: your chats help train the product or show ads. That is a fair trade only when you know it.

Paying does not automatically mean your chats are private. Some paid plans still use your chats for training, and some do not. The price tag is not a privacy promise. Read the data terms, not just the price.

The question is not free or paid. The question is: what does this tool do with my words, and can I change it?

Settings

The settings that matter

  • Training switch. Many tools have a setting like "use my chats to train models". Turn it off if you prefer your chats stay out of training.
  • Chat history. Some tools let you turn history off, so chats are not saved to your account. Check what "off" really means: it sometimes still stores them briefly.
  • Delete old chats. Remove chats you no longer need, especially ones with personal details.
  • Connected accounts. An AI connected to your email, calendar, or files can read them. Connect only what the job needs.
  • Sign out on shared devices. Your chats stay open until you sign out.

Settings move and get renamed. If you cannot find one, search the help pages for "training", "data", or "privacy". It is worth the two minutes.

Work and school

Work data has extra rules

  • Read your workplace or school policy first. Some forbid pasting any work material into outside AI tools, even with names removed.
  • Use approved tools. If your job provides an AI tool, use that one for work, not a personal account.
  • Client and student data is not yours to share. Names, grades, medical notes, and prices stay out of outside chats.
  • When in doubt, ask. A quick question to a manager beats a data leak you cannot undo.

Choose well

How to choose a tool

Before you trust a new AI tool with your words, ask four questions:

  1. Does it use my chats to train its models, and can I turn that off?
  2. Can I delete my chats, and does deleting really remove them?
  3. Where is my data stored, and who can read it?
  4. Does it connect to my email, files, or other accounts, and what can it do there?

If you cannot find the answers in the settings or the privacy page, treat that as an answer too, and pick a tool that explains itself plainly.

Before you type

Your data checklist

  • Did I check whether my tool uses my chats for training, and is the switch set the way I want?
  • Did I delete old chats I no longer need?
  • Did I check what accounts the AI is connected to?
  • For work or school, did I read the policy and use an approved tool?
  • Did I sign out on shared devices?
  • Am I choosing tools by their data terms, not just their price?

Remember: your data choices are habits, not one-time fixes. Settings change, tools change, and a monthly check keeps your choices current.

Common failure modes

  • "Delete means gone": deleting a chat does not always delete the data right away. Fix: clean prompts before sending, not after.
  • "Paid means private": price is not a privacy promise. Fix: read the data terms.
  • "It is just one chat": one chat can hold enough to identify you. Fix: treat every chat as stored.
  • "My data is not interesting": your words reveal your habits, contacts, and worries. Fix: decide what you share, do not assume no one cares.
  • "Settings are too hard to find": two minutes in settings beats a leak. Fix: search the help pages for "training" or "privacy".

Reference

Glossary: data terms

Short, plain definitions for the new terms used on this page.

Training data
The text, images, and other material an AI learns from. Some tools add your chats to it unless you opt out.
Data retention
How long a company keeps your chats and data after you send them.
Opt out
A setting that stops a company from using your chats to train its models.
Personal data
Any information that identifies you, such as your name, address, phone, email, or photo.
Sensitive data
Personal data that can cause real harm if exposed, such as passwords, ID numbers, bank details, or health records.
Privacy policy
A document that explains what a company does with your data. It is worth reading the data part, not the whole thing.
Two-factor authentication
A second check at sign in, usually a code from your phone, in addition to your password.
Encryption
Scrambling data so that only the intended reader can read it.
Data controls
Settings that let you change what a tool does with your data, such as training, history, and deletion.
Chat history
The saved list of your past chats in an AI tool.
Account
Your identity in a tool, which holds your chats, settings, and payment details.
Terms of service
The rules you agree to when you use a tool, including what the company may do with your data.
