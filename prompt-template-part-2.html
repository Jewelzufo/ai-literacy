Part 2 • Worked Examples

The prompt template: worked examples

In Part 1 you learned the template: Context, Role, Goal, Constraints. In Part 2 you use it. For every scenario, define your end goal first, then build the prompt that gets you there.

Start here

Step 0: Define your end goal first

Your end goal is the real result you want in your life, not just the thing you ask the AI to make. Write it before you touch the template. It decides everything else in the prompt.

Vague wish“Help me study.”
No finish line. The AI cannot tell when the job is done, so neither can you.
Clear end goal“By Friday I can answer biology quiz questions on cells without looking at my notes.”
You know what success looks like, and you can check whether you got it.

Three questions that define any end goal

  1. What will I have? Name the finished thing: a plan, an email ready to send, a list you can shop from.
  2. What will I use it for? Name the real use: pass the quiz, get the deadline moved, cook without stress.
  3. How will I know it is good enough? Name the test: short enough, polite enough, doable in the time and money you have.

Then build the prompt: your end goal becomes the Goal line, sharpened into an exact task. Context, Role, and Constraints are chosen to serve that end goal, nothing else.

CONTEXT: [Your situation and the background that matters]
ROLE: Act as [type of expert or helper]
GOAL: [Exactly what you want made or answered]
CONSTRAINTS: [Length, format, tone, and limits]

Note: the beginner guide calls Constraints by the plainer name Rules. It is the same part. How to use the examples below: each one starts from an end goal, then shows the four-part template filled in for that scenario. Read the end goal first, then see how each part of the template serves it.

Visual 1 of 6 • Learning

Scenario: Study for a quiz

The situation: You have a biology quiz on cells this Friday. You have 30 minutes a night and your class notes, but no plan.

The end goal first

End goalBy Friday I can answer quiz questions on cells without my notes, and I have practiced at least twice.

The filled template

ContextI have a biology quiz on cells on Friday and 30 minutes a night to study. I only have my class notes.
RoleAct as a patient study coach for beginners.
GoalMake a 4 day study plan and 10 practice questions with answers, so I am ready for the quiz on Friday.
ConstraintsEach session 30 minutes or less. Use a simple table for the plan. Plain words only.

What to notice: The Goal line points at Friday and the quiz, because the end goal does. A Goal that points anywhere else is a different job.

Visual 2 of 6 • Work

Scenario: Ask for more time at work

The situation: A report is due Wednesday, but a supplier sent the numbers late. You need to ask your manager for two more days, by email.

The end goal first

End goalMy manager agrees to a Friday deadline, and I have an email I can send today without sounding careless.

The filled template

ContextMy report is due Wednesday, but the supplier numbers arrived two days late, so I cannot finish it well by Wednesday.
RoleAct as a professional writing coach.
GoalDraft a short email asking to move the deadline to Friday, explaining the delay in one line.
ConstraintsUnder 120 words. Polite and calm. Do not blame the supplier. State the new date clearly.

What to notice: The end goal has two parts: the agreement, and the email. The Goal line covers both, and the Constraints keep the tone right.

Visual 3 of 6 • Food

Scenario: Dinners with no stress

The situation: You are new to cooking, you get home tired after work, and you keep ordering takeout because deciding is the hard part.

The end goal first

End goalI have 5 dinners decided for the week and one shopping list, so I cook at home instead of ordering takeout.

The filled template

ContextI am new to cooking and need simple dinners after work on weekdays. Deciding what to cook is the hard part.
RoleAct as a friendly cooking teacher for beginners.
GoalGive me 5 easy dinner ideas for this week and one combined shopping list.
ConstraintsEach meal under 30 minutes. Common ingredients only. Keep each recipe short.

What to notice: The end goal is decided dinners and one list, not a list of options. The Goal asks for exactly that, and the Constraints keep it doable on a tired evening.

Visual 4 of 6 • Career

Scenario: Prepare for a job interview

The situation: You have an interview on Tuesday for a customer service job. It is your first interview in two years and you freeze on common questions.

The end goal first

End goalBy Tuesday I can answer the common questions calmly in under a minute each, using only my real experience.

The filled template

ContextI have a customer service interview on Tuesday. It is my first interview in two years and I freeze on common questions.
RoleAct as a supportive interview coach.
GoalAsk me 6 common interview questions, one at a time, and help me shape short answers from my real experience.
ConstraintsOne question at a time. Each answer under 1 minute. Do not invent experience for me.

What to notice: The end goal names the test: calm, under a minute, real experience only. The Constraints repeat those three limits so the AI cannot drift from them.

Visual 5 of 6 • Planning

Scenario: Plan a birthday on a budget

The situation: You want to celebrate a friend on Saturday with 6 people, at home, and you can spend only a small set amount. You do not want a vague list of ideas.

The end goal first

End goalI have one decided plan for Saturday, with food, timing, and total cost settled, and nothing left to figure out on the day.

The filled template

ContextI am hosting a birthday at home on Saturday for 6 people, including me. My total budget is small and fixed.
RoleAct as a practical party planner who watches cost.
GoalMake one complete plan: a simple menu, a timeline for the day, and a shopping list with estimated costs that add up to my budget.
ConstraintsOne plan, not a list of options. Nothing that needs special equipment. Timeline by the hour.

What to notice: One plan, not options: the end goal says decided, and the Constraints say it again. When the end goal and the Constraints agree, the AI has nowhere else to go.

Visual 6 of 6 • Your turn

Your own scenario

The situation: Pick one real task from your own life this week. Small is fine. A real end goal beats a big vague one.

Your scenario, as a routine

  1. Pick one real task from your own lifeSmall is fine. A real end goal beats a big vague one.
  2. Write the end goalWhat you will have, what it is for, how you will know it worked.
  3. Fill the four partsContext, Role, Goal, Constraints, each one written from the end goal.
  4. Send it and save itUse the prompt on the real task, then keep it in your library.

Before you send

End goal checklist, for every scenario

  • Can I say my end goal in one sentence, with a finish line?
  • Does my Goal line ask for exactly what my end goal needs, nothing extra, nothing missing?
  • Did I include the shopping list, the date, the budget, or the other detail my end goal depends on?
  • Do my Constraints describe a result I can actually use: right length, right format, right tone?
  • Will I be able to tell, from the answer, whether my end goal was met?
  • If the answer is off, I change one part, usually Goal or Constraints, and resend. That is normal.

Remember: End goal first. Then Context + Role + Goal + Constraints = a prompt built to reach it.

Reference

Glossary: key terms

Short, plain definitions for every key term used on this page.

Context
The background part of a prompt: your situation and the details that matter.
Role
The helper or expert you ask the AI to act as, such as a teacher or coach.
Goal
The task line in a prompt: exactly what you want the AI to make or answer.
Constraints
The limits a prompt sets, such as length, format, tone, time, or things to avoid. Called Rules in the beginner guide.
End Goal
The real result you want in your life, and how you will know you got it.
Prompt
The full instruction you send to an AI, built here from the four template parts.
Template
A reusable structure with labeled blanks that you fill in for each new task.
Scenario
A worked example on this page: a real-life situation with its end goal and filled-in template.
Vague
Too unclear to act on, because key details like time, amount, or purpose are missing.
Format
The shape an answer takes, such as a table, a list, or a short email.
Tone
The feeling of the wording, such as polite, calm, friendly, or formal.
Checklist
A short list of questions you run through to catch missing details before you send.
