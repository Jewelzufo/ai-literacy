The prompt template: practice

In Part 1 you learned the template: Context, Role, Goal, Constraints. In Part 2 you use it. For every scenario, define your end goal first, then build the prompt that gets you there.

Start here

Step 0: Define your end goal first

Your end goal is the real result you want in your life, not just the thing you ask the AI to make. Write it before you touch the template. It decides everything else in the prompt.

Vague wish“Help me study.”
No finish line. The AI cannot tell when the job is done, so neither can you.
Clear end goal“By Friday I can answer biology quiz questions on cells without looking at my notes.”
You know what success looks like, and you can check whether you got it.

Three questions that define any end goal

  1. What will I have? Name the finished thing: a plan, an email ready to send, a list you can shop from.
  2. What will I use it for? Name the real use: pass the quiz, get the deadline moved, cook without stress.
  3. How will I know it is good enough? Name the test: short enough, polite enough, doable in the time and money you have.

Then build the prompt: your end goal becomes the Goal line, sharpened into an exact task. Context, Role, and Constraints are chosen to serve that end goal, nothing else.

CONTEXT: [Your situation and the background that matters]
ROLE: Act as [type of expert or helper]
GOAL: [Exactly what you want made or answered]
CONSTRAINTS: [Length, format, tone, and limits]

Note: the beginner guide calls Constraints by the plainer name Rules. It is the same part. How to use each activity below: do Step A, then Step B, then use Generate and Copy, then open the example and compare.

CONTEXT: I have a biology quiz on cells on Friday and 30 minutes a night to study. I only have my class notes.
ROLE: Act as a patient study coach for beginners.
GOAL: Make a 4 day study plan and 10 practice questions with answers, so I am ready for the quiz on Friday.
CONSTRAINTS: Each session 30 minutes or less. Use a simple table for the plan. Plain words only.

Check: Does your Goal line point at Friday and the quiz, or does it just say “help me study”? Fix the Goal line until it points at your end goal.

CONTEXT: My report is due Wednesday, but the supplier numbers arrived two days late, so I cannot finish it well by Wednesday.
ROLE: Act as a professional writing coach.
GOAL: Draft a short email asking to move the deadline to Friday, explaining the delay in one line.
CONSTRAINTS: Under 120 words. Polite and calm. Do not blame the supplier. State the new date clearly.

Check: Does your end goal name the reply you want, agreement to Friday, and not just “write an email”?

CONTEXT: I have a customer service interview on Tuesday. It is my first interview in two years and I freeze on common questions.
ROLE: Act as a supportive interview coach.
GOAL: Ask me 6 common interview questions, one at a time, and help me shape short answers from my real experience.
CONSTRAINTS: One question at a time. Each answer under 1 minute. Do not invent experience for me.

Check: Is practice in your prompt, or only a list of tips? Your end goal is being ready out loud, so ask for practice.

Check: Did you put your real budget amount in? A plan without a number cannot prove it fits your end goal.

Before you send

End goal checklist, for every scenario

  • Can I say my end goal in one sentence, with a finish line?
  • Does my Goal line ask for exactly what my end goal needs, nothing extra, nothing missing?
  • Did I include the shopping list, the date, the budget, or the other detail my end goal depends on?
  • Do my Constraints describe a result I can actually use: right length, right format, right tone?
  • Will I be able to tell, from the answer, whether my end goal was met?
  • If the answer is off, I change one part, usually Goal or Constraints, and resend. That is normal.

Remember: End goal first. Then Context + Role + Goal + Constraints = a prompt built to reach it.

Reference

Glossary: key terms

Short, plain definitions for every key term used on this page.

Context
The background part of a prompt: your situation and the details that matter.
Role
The helper or expert you ask the AI to act as, such as a teacher or coach.
Goal
The task line in a prompt: exactly what you want the AI to make or answer.
Constraints
The limits a prompt sets, such as length, format, tone, time, or things to avoid. Called Rules in the beginner guide.
End Goal
The real result you want in your life, and how you will know you got it.
Prompt
The full instruction you send to an AI, built here from the four template parts.
Template
A reusable structure with labeled blanks that you fill in for each new task.
Scenario
A practice situation on this page that you write a prompt for.
Vague
Too unclear to act on, because key details like time, amount, or purpose are missing.
Format
The shape an answer takes, such as a table, a list, or a short email.
Tone
The feeling of the wording, such as polite, calm, friendly, or formal.
Checklist
A short list of questions you run through to catch missing details before you send.
