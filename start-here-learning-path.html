New to AI? Start here, in order

This page is the map. One free course first, then the lessons in this repo, in order. No setup, no cost, no experience needed. Each lesson is a short page you can finish in one sitting.

The path

Your path, step by step

  1. Take the free AI Literacy course. A free, self-paced course from IBM SkillsBuild, about 4 hours, offered in multiple languages. It covers how AI works, its forms and enabling technologies, benefits and risks, AI ethics, and everyday and industry uses, with hands-on practice. Passing the quizzes at 80 percent or higher earns a shareable Credly badge. AI Literacy badge, IBM SkillsBuild. A free course I recommend.
  2. Read AI Safety Basics. AI Safety Basics. What never to paste into an AI chat, the 30-second check, account settings, and a brief ethics overview. Read this before you start prompting in earnest.
  3. Read AI Terms in Plain Terms. AI Terms in Plain Terms. 21 key terms explained in plain words, no math, no code. This is your reference for every word the course and the lessons use.
  4. Learn the prompt template. Part 1: The Prompt Template, On One Page, then Part 2: Worked Examples. The four-part template, Context, Role, Goal, Constraints, plus six worked examples that start from your end goal.
  5. Learn the prompting techniques. Part 3: Transforming Data, Part 4: Metaprompting, Part 5: Few-Shot Prompting, Part 6: Chain-of-Thought, and Part 7: Grounding with Your Own Context. One technique per page, each with worked visual examples.
  6. Keep going. Two related free badges continue where this path ends: Artificial Intelligence Fundamentals, which goes deeper into the technical side, and Generative AI in Action, which adds more prompt practice. Take them when you want more, not before.

The order matters. The course teaches the concepts. The safety page keeps you safe. The terms page gives you the words. The template and technique pages teach the doing. Skipping ahead works, but the order above is the shortest route from zero to confident.

How it fits together

Course concepts, repo examples

The course and this repo cover the same ground from opposite sides. The course explains, the repo shows you worked examples.

Course: how AI worksThe course explains what AI is, how it learns, and where it is used. The repo's terms page turns those ideas into plain words you can keep.
Course: benefits and risksThe course covers what can go right and wrong. The repo's safety page turns the risks into habits: the red list, the 30-second check, and account settings.
Course: AI ethicsThe course introduces ethics. The repo's safety page gives you five ideas to use: fairness, honesty, credit, consent, and impact.
Course: everyday usesThe course shows where AI fits in daily life. The repo's template and technique pages teach you to actually do it, with prompts you build yourself.

One possible first week

Your first week, at an easy pace

This is a suggestion, not a deadline. Move faster or slower, but keep the order.

  • Days 1 and 2: start the free course. Aim for the first half, about two hours.
  • Days 3 and 4: finish the course and take the quizzes. Then read the safety page.
  • Day 5: read the terms page, then Part 1. Build one prompt with the template.
  • Days 6 and 7: study the Part 2 worked examples. Pick the ones closest to your real life, and save your best prompts in a note.

After that, take one technique page at a time, Parts 3 to 7, whenever you have a free half hour. A technique only sticks when you use it on a real job, so bring a real job to each page.

Write your learning goal as a prompt

The job: Before you start the course, write down why you are learning AI, in one sentence, as a prompt you can reuse. A clear goal keeps the course from feeling like homework.

From a reason to a goal

Your reasonI want to write better emails at work and understand what AI can and cannot do.
→
One sentenceTurn that reason into one clear goal sentence, in plain words, no jargon.
→
Keep it visibleWrite the sentence on a note and keep it where you study.

What to notice: A vague goal like “learn AI” does not point anywhere. A goal that names what you will do, and for whom, does.
